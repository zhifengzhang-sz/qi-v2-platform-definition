# Decisions

Each decision once: its date, who decided (the owner, or the session), the decision, why, and the documents it shapes. The session's choices, on which the design is written and which are made with the owner, are listed apart, at the end.

- **2026-11-10, the owner.** The platform is defined on Kahn process networks: data is logs, the system's input and output; a component is a process, a function from its input logs to its output logs that never takes back output it has given; a platform is a network of components wired by the topics; the system is one function from the input logs to the output logs. Why: "the system is a map (in function sense) on the data". Shapes: `lean/Kahn/`, `lean/Workspace.lean`.
- **2026-11-10, the owner.** The Lean is a definition, not code to run: it is checked by its proofs, and `noncomputable` where the limit of logs needs it. Why: each component's code is written in its own project; the definition is what that code must satisfy. Shapes: `lean/`, `docs/acquisition.md`.
- **2026-11-10, the owner.** Every claim is proved from the base up. Why: a claim the logic does not force is a choice, and is named as one. Shapes: `lean/`, `agents/rules.md`.
- **2026-11-10, the owner; its reading that the contracts are the shape alone overturned, 2026-11-19, the owner.** The definitions give the shape and the contracts (the API). The shape is what each part reads and writes; the contracts are not that alone, but each contract's records and rules, which the definition holds (2026-11-19, the definition's two parts). Whether a part is a plugin is the implementation's, not the definition's. Shapes: `lean/Acquisition.lean`, `lean/Warehousing.lean`, `docs/acquisition.md`, `docs/designs/spec.md`.
- **2026-11-10, the owner.** Kafka's internals (replication, brokers) are taken as given. A topic has Kafka's shape, the same for every topic: one log of records for each partition, everything ever appended to it; what retention or compaction leave readable is a view of it. Why: Kafka's shape is well defined; and a view that replaces a record already given is not a process (`Kafka.compaction_not_process`). Shapes: `lean/Kafka.lean`.
- **2026-11-10, the session, the owner leaving it.** A topic's schema is its key and value types; a Lean type carries its fields' names, so nothing beyond the types is needed. Two topics with the same types have the same schema, and the warehouse consumer is the same for both, which is what "based on the topic schema" says. Shapes: `lean/Kafka.lean`, `lean/Warehousing.lean`.
- **2026-11-10, the owner.** The four components are acquisition, warehousing, visualization and the market model engine (optional), wired by the topics only. Acquisition is the one component that reads the system's input logs, the vendor's; the others read Kafka's topics alone. Shapes: `lean/Workspace.lean`, `lean/Wiring.lean`.
- **2026-11-10, the owner.** Each Kafka topic has one writer and any number of readers, and each table of the database one working space: no two topics have the same name, only warehousing writes into the database, and no table is written by two working spaces. Why: two writers into one log merge by arrival (`Kahn.no_arrival_merge`), which is a topic's reason, each partition of it being a log; for a table it is a choice (the decision on what a table is). A topic or a table is known by its name, so distinct names make it hold for Kafka's topics and the database's tables, not only for the definition's. Shapes: `lean/Workspace.lean`, `lean/Wiring.lean`.
- **2026-11-10, the owner; each of one series, of one signature, 2026-11-19, the owner (a working space is one series, below).** A working space has one acquisition; several working spaces may work side by side, one for each acquisition, each of one series, of one signature (Massive's daily bars of one ticker, say), each with its own warehousing, engine and visualization. Why: one acquisition to a working space keeps each working space simple; working side by side, they keep each topic's one writer and each table's one working space. Shapes: `lean/Wiring.lean` (`Workspaces`), `lean/Workspace.lean`, `lean/Acquisition.lean`.
- **2026-11-10, the owner; the k-line market's `KLine`, 2026-11-20, the owner (the contract specs in the Lean, below).** Acquisition is composed of the reader, the cleaner and the publisher; as it stood, each k-line contract defines one, its k-line the contract's, and since 2026-11-20 its k-line is market's `KLine`. - **2026-11-10, the owner.** Acquisition makes no corrected raw series and has no reset: a reset is outside the working space, and within one nothing starts again. Why: the reset's logic is too complicated for the working space. Shapes: `lean/Acquisition.lean`, `lean/Wiring.lean`, `docs/acquisition.md`.
- **2026-11-10, the owner.** The reader reads both parts of the raw series; how (a replayer in it, say) is the implementation's, not the definition's, and so is the switch from the first part to the second, by the reader's own state. ## Open

- **Which bar the cleaner refuses when a series would break market's order** (a bar dated 2099 among bars of today, say). The session's proposal: wait on a jump, one rule of one bound `b`, a parameter of the series: a bar within `b` past the last kept one is kept at once, one past it waits for the next bar to confirm or refuse it; `b = 0` is to wait always. Its reading: `docs/opening.md`; its research: `docs/designs/research/acquisition.md`, 2.2 (A series' order and dates).
- **The live candle**, a topic of market's `OngoingKLine`, a fifth kind beside the refused bars'. Its research: `docs/designs/research/acquisition.md`, 2.2 (Versions and duplicates of one bar), and `docs/designs/research/visualization.md`, 2.

## The session's choices

Each the session's, of 2026-11-19 or 2026-11-20, on which `docs/designs/spec.md` is written; none waits on the owner to be built. Design choices are made with the owner: where the owner chooses otherwise, the owner's decision is recorded above and the design and the Lean follow it. Two of them are faults of the session's design of 2026-11-19, fixed: a topic's partitions that no record is written to, and sets of topics of which a component uses one.

- **Each topic has one partition (2026-11-20).** Each topic of a working space has one partition, which holds the whole series of its kind, or the engine's results, in order; every reader reads that one log. Why: a working space is one series, and Kafka keeps order within a partition alone, which is how the design keeps the order of what is published (the owner's axioms of order, above). The session's design of 2026-11-19 put each series on one partition of its topic, its home, and left the topic's other partitions holding nothing, which was a fault: partitions no record is ever written to; and spreading a series over its topic's partitions by its `index` would have each reader put it back in order, for no gain to one series.
